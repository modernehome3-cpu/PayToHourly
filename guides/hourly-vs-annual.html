Hourly vs Annual Salary: How to Convert Hourly Pay to Yearly Income

Comparing an hourly wage with an annual salary can make job offers and pay rates easier to understand. If you know your hourly wage, you can estimate your annual gross income using a simple formula.

Hourly wage × hours per week × weeks per year = annual salary

A common example uses 40 hours per week and 52 weeks per year, which equals 2,080 working hours annually.

How to Convert Hourly Pay to Annual Salary

To estimate annual gross income from an hourly wage, multiply the hourly rate by your weekly working hours and then multiply by the number of weeks worked each year.

Annual salary = Hourly wage × Hours per week × Weeks per year

For a standard full-time schedule:

Annual salary = Hourly wage × 40 × 52

This gives 2,080 working hours per year.

How Many Hours Are in a Full-Time Work Year?

A common full-time salary calculation assumes 40 hours per week. With 52 weeks in a year:

40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours per year

This 2,080-hour figure is useful for estimating the hourly equivalent of an annual salary.

How to Convert an Annual Salary to Hourly Pay

You can also work in the opposite direction. If you know your annual salary, divide it by the estimated number of working hours in a year.

Hourly wage = Annual salary ÷ 2,080

For example, a $50,000 annual salary would be:

$50,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $24.04 per hour

This assumes a 40-hour workweek and 52 weeks per year.

Does Vacation Change the Calculation?

It can. The simple 2,080-hour calculation assumes 52 weeks of work. If you receive paid vacation, the annual salary remains the same while your actual hours worked may be lower.

If you take unpaid time off, however, your actual annual earnings can be lower than the simple estimate.

Gross Salary vs Take-Home Pay

Hourly-to-annual calculations generally show gross pay. Gross pay is the amount earned before taxes and other deductions.

Your take-home pay may be lower because of income taxes, Social Security, Medicare, insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions.

Why Compare Hourly and Annual Pay?

Comparing hourly and annual compensation can help when evaluating job offers. An annual salary may look higher, but the actual value can depend on working hours, overtime, paid time off and benefits.

Looking at both the hourly equivalent and annual amount gives you a clearer picture of the compensation package.
